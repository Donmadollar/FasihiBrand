I Wish Someone Could Understand the Things I Never Say

by Don Madollar

Sometimes, I wish people could look beyond the version of me they have come to know. Not because I want sympathy, but because there are parts of my story that I have never really found the courage to explain. There are battles I fought without telling anyone, disappointments I swallowed in silence, and moments that changed something inside me without anyone noticing. Perhaps if I could put all those experiences into words, people would understand why I sometimes withdraw, why I overthink simple things, why I can be surrounded by people and still feel alone. There is so much behind a person's behaviour that the world never gets to see.

I have learned that not everyone who is quiet is at peace, and not everyone who smiles has found a reason to be happy. Sometimes, people become careful because they have experienced what happens when they trust too easily. Some become independent because they learned that waiting for someone to show up can be disappointing. Others become distant, not because they dislike people, but because explaining their feelings has become more exhausting than keeping them to themselves.

What I find difficult is that I have so much understanding to offer, yet sometimes struggle to find the same understanding when I need it. I can sit with someone through their darkest moments, listen to their fears without judging them, and remind them that their past does not make them less deserving of love. But when it comes to my own struggles, I often find myself searching for the right words, wondering whether anyone would genuinely care enough to listen. I don't always need solutions. Sometimes, I just want someone who can stay present without making me feel like my emotions are a burden.

Perhaps that is one of the hardest parts of being human: wanting to be understood without having to explain every little thing. Wanting genuine connection in a world where most conversations barely scratch the surface. Wanting people who are willing to know you beyond your achievements, your jokes, your confidence and the version of yourself you present when everything seems fine.

I am slowly learning that I cannot expect everyone to understand the parts of me I have not shared, and I cannot keep waiting for someone to notice every struggle I hide. Maybe I also have to learn to speak, to open up, and to allow the right people to see me without feeling ashamed of my imperfections.

Still, I hope I never lose the ability to care deeply, to listen without judgment, or to make someone feel less alone. I hope life never makes me so guarded that I forget how to be kind.

Because beneath all the silence, the distance and the things left unexplained, there is still a person who wants genuine friendship, honest conversations and a place where they can finally exhale without pretending to be okay.

Sometimes, the greatest comfort is not finding someone who has all the answers, but finding someone in whose presence you no longer feel the need to hide your questions.
